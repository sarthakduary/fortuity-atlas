02 / ABOUT THE ATLAS

A small distinction.
A rich landscape.

From black-hole horizons to the algebra of finite matrices.

THE ALGEBRAIC LENS

HQ = ker Q / im Q

Supersymmetric states, organized by what survives as the rank grows.

Read the founding perspective ↗

AdS/CFT turns the question of what a black hole is made of into a question about an ordinary quantum system: which states of the boundary theory are its microstates, and how do they account for its entropy?

Counting states is only part of the challenge. We also want to understand what distinguishes a typical black-hole microstate from a gas of gravitons. Supersymmetry makes this question tractable. For a chosen nilpotent supercharge Q, with Q2 = 0, the corresponding BPS states are represented by its cohomology: Q-closed states, modulo Q-exact ones. This retains the individual bosonic and fermionic states that can cancel in a signed supersymmetric index.

A weak-gravity thought experiment

Imagine keeping the charges and angular momenta fixed while weakening gravity. In the holographic large-N limit, the Newton constant in AdS units tends to zero. A smooth horizonless configuration is expected to have a gentle limit: viewed as a graviton coherent state, it can resolve into gravitons whose interactions become weaker.

A black-hole horizon behaves differently. The Bekenstein–Hawking relation, S = A / (4GN), says that if the entropy is held fixed, the horizon area must shrink as gravity is turned off. There is no regular limit preserving a finite-area horizon. This is a physical motivation for the distinction below, rather than a claim that fixed charges automatically keep the entropy unchanged.

What survives as the rank grows?

Monotone

A monotone class admits a representative that is Q-closed before imposing finite-N trace relations. It therefore has a natural continuation to larger ranks, much as a graviton state should.

Fortuitous

A fortuitous class depends essentially on finite-rank identities. In matrix examples, its Q-variation vanishes because of a trace relation for small matrices. At sufficiently large rank that relation no longer holds, obstructing its continuation as a BPS class.

Its supersymmetry is, in this sense, an accident of finite rank. The central conjecture is that typical supersymmetric black-hole microstates are fortuitous, while monotone states describe the smooth horizonless sector. This turns a question about horizons into a concrete program: construct finite-N cohomology classes, count them, understand their relations, and test whether their growth accounts for black-hole entropy.

Fortuity Atlas brings together this developing story across super-Yang–Mills theory, matrix quantum mechanics, and related holographic models. The collection includes direct studies of fortuity and closely connected foundational work in arXiv’s hep-th category.

Scope & inclusion criteria +

Entries are selected from searches for “fortuity,” “fortuitous,” and related BPS-cohomology terminology in titles and abstracts, together with relevant papers identified through references. Core papers directly investigate these questions; background papers supply important context. This is a reviewed bibliography, not a guarantee that every relevant paper has been captured.
